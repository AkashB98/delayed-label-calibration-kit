# delayed-label-calibration-kit

**When the labels arrive months late, the obvious evaluation lies — this kit measures how much, corrects it with a standard life-table completion, and says plainly which cohorts you may not claim yet.**

In collections and credit, a loan booked today may not show its default for six months or more. Score that book now, treating every not-yet-defaulted loan as a good loan, and the default rate looks reassuringly low — not because the book is good, but because most of its bad outcomes have not surfaced yet. This repo builds that trap on a seeded synthetic loan book with planted label lags, then builds the honest way out: a completion curve learned only from cohorts old enough to be fully labeled, projections for the young cohorts with confidence intervals that widen as maturity falls, and a per-cohort maturity report that marks each vintage **claimable**, **provisional**, or **not claimable**.

> **Synthetic data only.** Every borrower is fictional, generated by a seeded generator (seed 42) for a fictional lender, Meridian Sample Lending. No real borrower, account, or portfolio data anywhere.

Stdlib-only Python 3. Zero third-party imports, zero network, zero API keys.

## The headline: the naive number is wrong by 5.5 points on the slow segment

Planted truth: the **fast** segment ultimately defaults at **0.08** and surfaces its defaults by month-on-book 2; the **slow** segment ultimately defaults at **0.18** and surfaces them slowly, complete only by month-on-book 8. Twelve monthly cohorts, 1–12 months old at the snapshot, 500 loans per segment per cohort (12,000 loans).

| Segment | Planted truth | Naive (unseen = good) | Maturity-filtered (mature cohorts only) | Completion projection (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast | 0.08 | 0.074 (bias −0.006) | 0.075818 — excludes 1 cohort | **0.075926** [0.069097, 0.082755] — covers truth |
| slow | 0.18 | **0.125 (bias −0.055)** | 0.1672 — excludes **7 of 12 cohorts** | **0.176126** [0.158605, 0.193646] — covers truth |

Read the three arms as three attitudes:

1. **Naive** is current and wrong: on the slow segment it understates the default rate by 5.5 points, exactly the planted delay at work.
2. **Maturity-filtered** is right about the cohorts it touches and silent about everything recent — for the slow segment it throws away the newest seven vintages, which are usually the ones anyone is asking about.
3. **Completion** is current *and* honest: it projects each young cohort through the completion curve and reports an interval, not a point of false precision. The interval widens as a cohort's maturity drops (pinned in eval G4: CI width 0.047624 at full completion vs 0.102791 at 25% completion, same underlying rate and sample size).

## The label-maturity report

The headline artifact. For each cohort it estimates what fraction of its ultimate defaults have already surfaced (observed defaults ÷ completion-projected ultimate defaults) and labels it: **claimable** at ≥ 0.95 arrived, **provisional** at ≥ 0.50, **not claimable** below that — each row stating in words what can and cannot be claimed.

| Cohort | Age (months) | Labels arrived | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-07 → 2024-11 | 12 → 8 | 1.0 | claimable (×5) |
| 2024-12 | 7 | 0.962356 | claimable |
| 2025-01 | 6 | 0.886327 | provisional |
| 2025-02 | 5 | 0.785815 | provisional |
| 2025-03 | 4 | 0.621161 | provisional |
| 2025-04 | 3 | 0.524312 | provisional |
| 2025-05 | 2 | 0.355871 | not claimable |
| 2025-06 | 1 | 0.322304 | not claimable |

Summary: **6 claimable / 4 provisional / 2 not claimable**. The labels are not vibes — eval G6 recomputes them from the planted arrival distributions and they agree cohort by cohort.

## An honest note on the method

The completion method here is **not new, and this repo does not present it as new**. It is the standard actuarial life-table / cumulative-incidence technique — chain-ladder-style development, long used for insurance claim development and credit vintage analysis: learn from matured cohorts what share of ultimate outcomes has typically appeared by each age, then gross up young cohorts by that share. The contribution of this kit is the discipline around the standard method: curves built *only* from matured cohorts, intervals that widen as maturity falls, three arms scored against known truth so the naive lie is measured rather than asserted, and a claimability report that refuses to let a young cohort be quoted as final.

## What this does NOT do

Plainly, and by design:

- **No drift or PSI monitoring** — population-drift detection is a neighbouring problem with its own kits; a label-delay bias is not drift and is not measured as drift here.
- **No retrain trigger and no model at all** — there is no scoring model in this repo, so there is no AUC / model-performance pack. The subject is the *evaluation of a book under delayed labels*, not model quality.
- **No causal or policy claims** — a completion projection estimates an ultimate rate; it does not say why the rate is what it is, or what changing underwriting would do.

## Quickstart

```bash
python3 cli.py generate                        # dataset summary (seed 42)
python3 cli.py report                          # curves + three arms vs truth + maturity
python3 cli.py maturity                        # the label-maturity report only
python3 cli.py check --scenario delayed; echo $?  # 1 = delayed-label bias detected
python3 cli.py check --scenario instant; echo $?  # 0 = no delay, no bias (control)
python3 cli.py serve --port 8765               # GET /health, /report, /maturity on 127.0.0.1
python3 demo.py                                # end to end, incl. live serve round-trip
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests          # 47 tests
python3 evals/run_evals.py                     # 10 golden evals
```

`check` exit codes: **0** = no material delayed-label bias (naive and completion agree within 0.02 in every segment), **1** = bias detected — the naive rate materially understates a segment and must not be claimed, **2** = data/IO error (e.g. a named input file does not exist).

## POC framing

If this were a customer's acceptance criteria for a collections-analytics engagement, the conversation would go like this:

- **Problem:** the business quotes default rates on recent vintages whose labels have mostly not arrived, so every recent book looks better than it is, and decisions (limits, collections staffing, provisioning) get made on numbers that will only move one way — up.
- **Success criteria, declared up front:** (1) quantify the naive bias against a known ground truth rather than arguing about it; (2) a corrected estimate for *current* vintages whose interval actually covers the truth; (3) uncertainty that visibly grows as a cohort gets younger; (4) a per-cohort statement of what may be claimed, so a stakeholder cannot accidentally quote a provisional number as final.
- **Result:** all four criteria met on the seeded book — naive bias measured at −0.055 on the slow segment, completion intervals covering planted truth in both segments, CI width more than doubling from full to quarter completion, and a 6 / 4 / 2 claimability split that matches the planted arrival fractions exactly.

## How it works

**The generator** (`generator.py`) plants everything the evals later check: per-segment ultimate rates (fast 0.08, slow 0.18) and per-segment arrival distributions over months on book (fast: 70% at MOB 1, 30% at MOB 2; slow: cumulative 5% / 15% / 30% / 50% / 70% / 85% / 95% / 100% by MOB 1–8). Each record carries the ground truth (`ultimate_default`) and the snapshot view (`observed_default`, `arrival_mob` when surfaced). An `instant` control scenario surfaces every default at MOB 1, so the check gate can prove it stays silent when there is no delay.

**The completion curve** (`calibration.py`) is built per segment from matured cohorts only — age ≥ the segment horizon (2 for fast, 8 for slow) — as defaults-arrived-by-each-MOB over all defaults in those cohorts. The estimated slow curve tracks the planted one closely (0.055 / 0.134 / 0.278 / 0.469 / 0.701 / 0.849 / 0.950 / 1.0 against planted 0.05 / 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.50 / 0.70 / 0.85 / 0.95 / 1.0). Estimation code reads only observed fields; `ultimate_default` is read in exactly one place, to score the arms against truth.

**The projection** divides a group's observed rate by its completion share at its age; the 95% normal-approximation interval on the observed rate divides by the same share — which is precisely why younger cohorts get wider intervals. Segment-level completion pools the per-group projections and their variances.

## Scope and honest limits

- **Synthetic data, planted lags.** The arrival distributions are built in at known shapes, so "the completion covers truth" proves the machinery, not that real label lags look like this. Real books have segment definitions, censoring, and cure behaviour that a planted book does not.
- **The curve assumes the past's lag is the future's lag.** Completion projects young cohorts using how matured cohorts developed. If the lag itself changes (a new collections process, a reporting change), the curve is wrong in a way this kit does not detect — that detection is a monitoring problem, deliberately out of scope.
- **Normal-approximation intervals.** Intervals are a simple binomial normal approximation divided by completion — readable and honest about widening, but a production version would use a variance model that also carries curve-estimation uncertainty from the matured cohorts.
- **Two segments, one snapshot.** Real maturity reporting runs per product, channel, and risk band, refreshed as the snapshot moves; the thresholds (0.95 / 0.50) are stated POC conventions, not recommendations.
- **Defaults are the only label.** The same machinery applies to any delayed binary outcome (charge-offs, fraud confirmations, churn), but nothing here handles competing risks or partial recoveries.

## Why this project

Forward-deployed analytics work is full of numbers that are technically computed and substantively false, and delayed labels are the quietest cause: nobody made an error, the outcomes simply have not happened *yet*. This repo makes that failure mode measurable, corrects it with a standard method applied carefully, and — the part that matters most in a customer room — states, cohort by cohort, what you are not allowed to claim yet.
